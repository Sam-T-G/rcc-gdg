Meet someone · Pairs · One minute each

Your story so far, in one minute.

1:00

Where you started. Where you are. Where you’re headed. T to start, R for the second person.

Pair with someone you don’t know. Run the timer twice, once per person. Odd number: make a three at forty seconds each. If the room is warm and there’s time, rotate partners once and run it again; the second telling is always tighter, which sets up the next slide.

Hands up

Whose story do you still remember?

Now say why. What made it stick?

Two or three answers. Listen for the pattern and say it back: a specific detail, a number, a person, a turn in the story. Almost nobody remembers the adjectives.

Storytelling is signaling

People believe what you show them, not what you claim.

Every story you just told was sending signals about you, on purpose or not.

Signaling: the qualities people read off what you choose to say, not the qualities you name. It works the same at a party, in office hours, in an interview, and in a pitch. What you lead with, what you skip, and what you sound sure about all get read.

Claim it, or signal it.

Claiming

“I’m really passionate about healthcare.”

Signaling

“I rewrote the campus clinic’s intake form. Check-in went from eleven minutes to four.”

Same example as the landing page, and it’s an example, not a real person. The second never says “passionate,” “hardworking,” or “takes initiative,” and signals all three. That’s the whole semester in one line.
