This arc

Get understood by someone with no reason to listen.

This week is the install: the mechanics, at the lowest stakes, with a partner you picked. Next week adds a listener who doesn’t want to hear it, the week after makes it about something you actually want, and the last week sends it to a real person.

Three mechanics

  1. Specific beats important.“I want to know why the clinic intake form takes eleven minutes” starts a conversation.
  2. A number beats an adjective.“Forty-one people through the same chemistry unit” can be asked about. “A lot” can’t.
  3. Name a person, not a category.Who’s worse off if you never do this? Say them.

Leave this slide up for the whole Rep. Press once per mechanic.

  1. Um, so I’m a nursing major, I’m in anatomy and pharm right now, and I kind of work part time at the campus clinic, and I’ve been helping them fix their paperwork, which has honestly been pretty interesting.
  2. I’m a nursing major, and I work part time at the campus clinic, and I’ve been helping them fix their paperwork, which has been pretty interesting.
  3. I work at the campus clinic. Check-in used to take eleven minutes, and I rewrote the intake form, and now it’s four.

Press once per version. Words on the way out strike through, then close up; new words arrive in green. Same example as the club landing page. It’s an example, not a script.

Debrief

What did the room hear that you didn’t say?

Then: which mechanic was hardest to add? Almost always the number, because it requires having counted something. Then: what would you have to go do this month so that a better number exists by December? That last one seeds the Evidence arc. Let it sit; don’t answer it.
