## What changed from the card, and why

The card is a pure hook: a 60-minute rep with no intro, because it was built to work on strangers
who have not committed to anything. You want intro, timeline, and officer recruitment in the same
hour. Those pull in opposite directions, so the resolution is to keep the rep as the spine and
buy the club block out of the phase margins rather than out of the rep.

What that costs, phase by phase:

| Phase | Card | Today | What is lost |

| The Rep | 18 | 16 | Movement drops from 12 minutes to 11 |

| Debrief and rung | 10 | 9 | One fewer answer taken per question |
| **Club block** | 0 | **11** | new |

The five laws still hold, which is the test that matters:

- **Law 1**, everyone speaks in the first twelve minutes: warm rep runs 2:34 to 2:39, so the last
  person speaks at 2:39. The law says 2:42.
- **Law 2**, no deck over five slides: the deck scaffold at the bottom is five.

  never behind.

## The club block (3:14, 11 min)

Placement is deliberate. It comes right after the reveal, where the card already says "everything we
do this semester is the same move you just made, aimed at your own life." The club block is the
continuation of that sentence, not an interruption of it.

### Part 1: What this club is (3 min)

**One sentence.** GDG on Campus at RCC is a Google Developer Group chapter, and the weekly meeting
is a practice floor for the things no class assigns: saying what you want, asking someone with power
for it, and making sure your work is visible to people who were not there.

**The part that surprises people, and it just happened to them.** This is deliberately not a coding
club. A nursing, business, art, or undeclared student should never be the least equipped person in
the room. You do not have to explain that as a policy, because the room spent forty minutes proving
it.

**For the programmers, ten seconds, then move on.** Every session ends by naming the technical
version of the skill and never teaching it. Today's was filing an issue on a project you do not own.
Twenty-second answers become the first paragraph of a README. Taking critique becomes a code review.
That is the ladder, and it is how the club is an on-ramp without becoming a CS club.

**Where the Google part shows up.** The chapter is on Bevy at gdg.community.dev, which is where
events are published and checked in. Technical things do exist, DevFest, the Modular Impact Sprint,
guest speakers, and they are separate opt-in events, never the weekly meeting. Say that rule out
loud; it is the promise that keeps the room mixed.

**Numbers, if asked:** founded Spring 2026, 26 people on the Bevy chapter page as of 2026-08-24,
23 active members on the Spring 2026 funding request.

### Part 2: The semester (4 min)

Do not read the table. Say the shape, then leave it on screen.

Fourteen Thursdays, two hooks and three arcs of four. Each arc takes one skill through the same four
stages: install it, add pressure, aim it at your own real situation, then ship it outside the room.

| Arc | Dates | What it is | What you leave with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hooks | Sep 3, Sep 10 | Today, then the line you say at Club Rush | A sentence about yourself that works cold |
| **Voice** | Sep 17 to Oct 8 | Saying what you want without decorating it | **Oct 8: a real message, actually sent, in the room** |
| **Ask** | Oct 15 to Nov 5 | Asking someone who can say no | **Nov 5: a booked appointment with someone with power** |
| **Evidence** | Nov 12 to Dec 10 | Making work visible to people who were not there | **Dec 10, Demo Day: three minutes in front of outsiders** |

Dark Thursdays: Nov 26 in the no-class week, Dec 17 in finals.

The line that sells the semester, and it is true: every arc ends with a receipt made in this room,
not assigned as homework. On October 8 you do not learn about cold outreach, you send the message
before you leave. On November 5 you do not learn about office hours, you book the appointment.

**Every session works cold.** Nobody is behind if they miss three weeks. That is a design rule, not
a courtesy, so say it to the person who is worried about committing.

### Part 3: The seats (4 min)

Frame this as a rotation, not an election, because that is what it actually is.

**How this club is staffed.** Session cards name a seat, never a person. One table maps seats to
people, so a swap is one row and the cards get reused next year. Right now `LEAD` is the only seat
filled.

| Seat | What it costs | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| `FAC-A` to `FAC-C` | Facilitate a session from the card. The card is a script | 2 sessions each |
| `FAC-D` | The lightest seat, on purpose. Built for someone brand new | 1 session |
| `NOTE` | Notes and Bevy check-in. Ten minutes a week | Every week |
| `FLOAT-1`, `FLOAT-2` | Circulate during a ship week so the facilitator can run the clock | Oct 8 and Nov 5 |
| `TIME` | Run the clock and nothing else | Dec 10 |

**The pacing rule that makes this safe, and say it, because it is the whole reason someone says yes:**
nobody runs a session before backing one up twice. Your first time holding the card in front of the
room is never your first time in the room. And nobody runs two weeks in a row.

**So today's ask is not "be an officer."** It is "back up a session." Standing next to the person
running it, holding a copy of the card, doing nothing. That is the entry point, and `FAC-D` is the
seat that grows into a heavier one in spring.

**The officer roles that are separate from facilitator seats,** and the club needs them for RCC
recognition: Vice President, Treasurer, Secretary, ICC Representative, Faculty Advisor, and a second
repo admin. RCC requires seven fee-paid students including a President, a Treasurer, and an ICC
Representative, plus a full-time faculty advisor. The ICC seat is a standing Monday 9:30 AM
commitment and three absences costs the club its standing, so do not undersell it.

Names go on a sheet today. They go in the repo only with written consent, per the privacy policy.

## Failure modes from the card, worth rereading

- Someone writes a joke card. Deal it anyway. Do not police the cards.
- Someone writes something heavy. The anonymity is what makes that safe. Do not single it out during
  live fire; pick the three live-fire cards from people who look comfortable.
- The room sits down and talks. The rep dies sitting. Keep everyone standing for the full eleven
  minutes.
- Cards leave the room. They do not.
