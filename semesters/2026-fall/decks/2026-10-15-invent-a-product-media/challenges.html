For organizers · October 15

Challenge cards

Walk up to a group and ask what kind of idea they have. Tap that kind, draw a card, and read it to them. They pick a fix and write it down; it goes in their pitch.

    Any idea

    Tap “Draw a card.”

    Every card is listed below, grouped by the kind of idea.

    Any idea

    1. Look it up right now. Does it already exist? What does yours do that it doesn’t?
    2. Who would hate this idea? What would they do about it?
    3. What’s the smallest version you could test this week without building anything?
    4. A thousand people use it tomorrow. What breaks first?
    5. Why hasn’t someone already done this? Is there a reason?

    An app

    1. Why would someone download an app for this instead of using what they already have?
    2. They used it once. Why would they open it again next week?
    3. Their phone is dead, or there’s no signal where they need it. Now what?
    4. Who keeps it updated after your group graduates?

    Connects two groups of people

    Riders and drivers, buyers and sellers, tutors and students.

    1. Which side shows up first? Nobody joins an empty app.
    2. Someone gets scammed or doesn’t show up. What happens, and who’s responsible?
    3. Why would people keep using it instead of trading numbers and leaving?
    4. One side has way more people than the other. Who waits, and for how long?

    Uses data or location

    1. Where does your data come from, and who keeps it right?
    2. The data is wrong once and someone misses class. Do they trust it again?
    3. Would you want this collected about you? What do you promise your users?
    4. Someone uses it to track a person who doesn’t want to be found. How do you stop that?

    A new rule

    1. Who has the power to make this rule? Have you ever talked to them?
    2. Who loses if the rule passes? Will they fight it?
    3. How would anyone know if people follow it?
    4. The people in charge say “we tried that already.” What do you say back?

    A physical thing

    A sign, a sensor, a kiosk, a locker.

    1. Who pays to install it, and who fixes it when it breaks?
    2. It gets rained on, kicked, or stolen. Then what?
    3. Who has to say yes before you can put it on campus?
    4. It needs power or Wi-Fi. Where does that come from?

    People do the work

    A shuttle, valet, tutoring, delivery.

    1. Who does the work, and why do they keep doing it after week two?
    2. Your best worker quits. What happens to your users?
    3. How do you make sure it’s done well every time?
    4. Everyone needs it at the same hour. How many people do you need then?

    Money

    1. Would you pay for this yourself? How much, exactly?
    2. It costs money to run every month. Where does that money come from in month one?
    3. A free version shows up. Why do people still pay for yours?
    4. Who pays: users, a business, the school, or ads? What happens if they stop?
