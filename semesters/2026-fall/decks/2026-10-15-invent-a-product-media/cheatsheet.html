Cheat sheet · Thursday, October 15

Invent a product

Everything from today on one page. No code needed. Your group picks a real problem, finds out why it happens, and invents a fix.

  1. 1 Problem
  2. 2 Why
  3. 3 Ideas
  4. 4 Build
  5. 5 Checks
  6. 6 Pitch
1

Pick a problem5 minutes, in your group

Everyone names one thing that annoyed them this week, big or small. Write them all down, then pick one you’ve felt yourself.

Stuck? Try one of these:

  • Parking before class
  • Scalpers and concert tickets
  • Textbook access codes
  • Getting into a full class
  • Splitting bills with roommates
  • A quiet place to study
  • One person doing the whole group project
  • Food thrown out while students skip meals

Check: Who do you know who has this problem? If nobody, pick another one.

2

Find out why it happens8 minutes, one question at a time

  1. Who has it? Be exact.
  2. When is it worst?
  3. What do they do about it now?
  4. Why hasn’t anyone fixed it?

Then put it in one sentence:

___ can’t ___ because ___.

Example · parking

Students with 9:00 classes can’t find a spot because they can’t see which ones are open.

Example · tickets

Fans can’t buy at face value because bots buy faster than any person can click.

The “because” is where your ideas come from. If the because is “nobody can see the open spots,” the fix is showing people the spots, not building a garage.

Check: How do you know? Did someone tell you, or are you guessing? Ask why one more time.

3

Get eight ideas (Crazy 8s)8 minutes, on your own

Fold a sheet of paper in half three times so it has eight boxes. Put one idea in each box, about a minute each. Words or doodles both count. No talking until time’s up.

When you get stuck:

Borrow it.
How would an airline, a restaurant, or a dating app handle this?
Pay someone.
Who would fix this for money? Now it’s a business.
Change a rule.
Could a new rule fix it with no app at all?
Skip the step.
What if they never had to do the annoying part?
Go weird.
At least one of your eight can’t be an app.
Example · eight parking ideas

Live map of open spots (app) · signs at each gate with the open count (sign) · valet parking (business) · carpool matches by class time (app) · staggered class start times (rule) · a shuttle from the far lot (service) · reserve a spot the night before (app) · premium spots near the buildings that cost more (business).

4

Pick one and build it out6 minutes, in your group

  1. Share your best two ideas. Everyone gets two votes. Mixing two ideas is fine.
  2. Give it a name.
  3. How does it work? Three steps, from the user’s side: what they tap, see, or do.
  4. Who pays for it? The users, a business, the school, or ads.

Check: Look it up. Does it already exist? That’s normal. What does yours do that the other one doesn’t, or who does it miss?

5

Challenge checks5 minutes, in your group

Real life asks these. Pick the one that hurts your idea most, change your idea to handle it, and write down what you changed. It goes in your pitch.

Who are your first 10 users, and where do you find them?

It costs money to run every month. Who pays?

The school says no. What’s plan B?

A big company copies you. Why do people stay with yours?

Would people trust it with their info? What do you promise them?

An organizer might also come by with a challenge made for your idea.

6

Pitch it with STAR5 minutes to plan, 60 seconds to pitch

Same STAR as last week, used for a pitch instead of a story. Spend most of your time on the A.

S

Situation. Who has the problem, and when?

T

Task. What has to change? Say your because.

A

Action. What you built, in three steps.

R

Result. What gets better for them, and who pays.

Then name one challenge you fixed. After your pitch, the room gets one question. Then everyone votes on which product they’d actually use.

Before next Thursday

Ask one person who has your problem if they’d use your idea.

Bring back what they said, even if it’s no.
