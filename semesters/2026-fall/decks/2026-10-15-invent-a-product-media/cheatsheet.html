Step 2 of 6 · 8 minutes

Find out why it happens

Answer one at a time. The last one matters most.

Your problem

Put it in one sentence

Your sentence

___ can’t ___ because ___.

See two examples

Parking. Students with 9:00 classes can’t find a spot because they can’t see which ones are open.

Tickets. Fans can’t buy at face value because bots buy faster than any person can click.

The “because” is where your ideas come from. If nobody can see the open spots, the fix is showing people the spots, not building a garage.

  1. If your “why” is “nobody cares,” ask who would have to care, and why they don’t.

  2. Check: How do you know? Did someone tell you, or are you guessing?

  3. Ask “why” one more time. The first answer is usually not the real one.

See eight parking ideas

A live map of open spots (an app) · signs at each gate with the open count (a sign) · valet parking (a business) · carpool matches by class time (an app) · staggered class start times (a rule) · a shuttle from the far lot (a service) · reserving a spot the night before (an app) · premium spots near the buildings that cost more (a business).

Only three of the eight are apps.

  1. Borrow it. How would an airline, a restaurant, or a dating app handle this?

  2. Pay someone. Who would fix this for money? Now it’s a business.

  3. Change a rule. Could a new rule fix it with no app at all?

  4. Skip the step. What if they never had to do the annoying part?

  5. Check: At least one of your eight can’t be an app.
