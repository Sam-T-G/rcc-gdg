The part that made it possible wasn’t the code.

How we did it

We ran it like a small company.

Each person owned one part. One page kept everyone on the same idea.

Examples from Gus: one person on the face check, one on the backend, one on the iPhone app, one on the robot’s body, and one page (the scope doc) that said what was in, what was out, and what came next. Every big decision got written down, so nobody had to ask twice.

Real tech companies run on the same idea, just bigger.

How a product team ships

  1. Find the problem.Who has it, and why does it keep happening?
  2. Write it down.One page everyone builds from.
  3. Build in small steps.Something that runs, then something better.
  4. Review, then launch.Fresh eyes before real people see it.

At a big company each step has its own people: researchers, product managers, designers, engineers, testers, privacy reviewers, and marketing. Two weeks ago you did step one, finding the problem. Today you do all four in 45 minutes.

We shrink that company down to one table.

Your team is a tiny company.

Your seat · the job at a real company

  1. Lead · the engineering manager
  2. PM · the product manager
  3. Designer · the UX designer
  4. QA + Trust · testing and privacy
  5. Launch · product marketing

The AI agent is the engineering team. The lead runs it, and everyone else decides what it builds. Each seat has a card; the next five slides are those cards, about 30 seconds each. The printed cards carry the detail.

Start with the seat that holds the team together.

The PM keeps everyone on one idea.

Product manager · in priority order

  1. Who it’s for. Every change serves them.
  2. One must-do. Everything else waits.
  3. The team sheet matches what’s built.
  4. Call the clock. Cut scope, not time.
  5. Split team? Decide in 30 seconds.

The PM owns the team sheet and the next instruction for the agent. When the build and the sheet disagree, stop and fix the sheet first.

Handoff line if you move: “We’re building ___ for ___. The next ask is ___. We cut ___.”

Next, the seat that decides what people actually see.

The designer owns the screen.

UX designer · in priority order

  1. One screen, sketched on paper first.
  2. Write every word on the screen.
  3. A stranger gets it in 5 seconds.
  4. Plan for loading, empty, and errors.

The designer owns the paper sketch and the “On screen” line of the team sheet. The agent builds whatever words it’s given, so plain words on paper become plain words in the app.

Handoff line: “The screen is this sketch. The words are in the sheet. The weak spot is ___.”

Then someone has to try to break it.

QA + Trust finds what breaks first.

Testing and privacy · in priority order

  1. Three tests with real input.
  2. What does it see, send, and keep?
  3. What if someone uses it wrong?
  4. Run the demo once before freeze.

Real input means the real camera, a real photo, a real search. Not “it looks right.” The second line is the same question we asked about Gus: what happens to someone’s face, photo, or location after the app sees it?

Handoff line: “Tests 1 and 3 pass, 2 fails on ___. The demo path is ___.”

And someone has to make the room care.

Launch sells it in two minutes.

Product marketing · in priority order

  1. Open with the person and their problem.
  2. Let the demo do most of the talking.
  3. End on what changed for them.
  4. Rehearse once with QA’s demo path.

This is STAR again from 10-08, with a live demo as the A. The R is what’s different for the person after.

Handoff line: “The pitch opens with ___ and ends with ___. ___ speaks.”

Last seat, the one at the keyboard.

The lead keeps the agent busy.

Engineering manager · one per team

  1. The app always runs.
  2. The agent never sits idle.
  3. Stuck for a minute? Cut it.
  4. Say out loud what you tell the agent.

Leads are the officers running agents in the live repo. Small changes only; if a change breaks the app, roll it back and tell the PM. Leads don’t switch teams; the laptop stays put.

So how does everyone stay in sync while things move this fast?

One sheet keeps everyone aligned.

The team sheet · the agent reads it too

  1. For · one real person
  2. Problem · what goes wrong today
  3. Must do · the one thing it does
  4. Not doing · what we cut
  5. Next ask · the agent’s next job

It lives as TEAM.md in each team’s folder, and a printed copy sits on the table. The full sheet also has “On screen” (the designer’s words), “Tests” (QA’s three checks), and “Who’s here.” The agent is told to read it before every pass, so the people and the agent build from the same page.

That sheet is also what lets you move.

Anyone can switch teams.

  1. Move at a break.Or say your handoff line first.
  2. Take the open seat.Roles stay with the team.
  3. Read the sheet.You’re caught up in 30 seconds.
  4. QA tests another team.Fresh eyes during review.

If an idea from one team helps another, walk it over. If a seat empties, it merges: on a team of four, the PM also does Launch; on a team of three, the designer also does QA. The leaving person’s handoff line goes on the sheet.

Three briefs. Pick the one that pulls you in.

Pick a brief.

  1. EyesThe laptop camera tracks hands, faces, or poses. Nothing leaves the laptop.
  2. WordsGemini reads a photo or some text and hands back clean data.
  3. PlacesEvents from Ticketmaster on a map, with the weather.

Each brief fixes the tech and leaves the problem open. The team picks who it’s for. Starting ideas, only if a team is stuck: Eyes, count raised hands or flip slides with a gesture. Words, turn a photo of a flyer into a calendar event. Places, find something to do near campus this weekend.

Under the hood: Eyes uses MediaPipe in the browser, no key. Words uses the Gemini API with a key from AI Studio. Places uses the Ticketmaster Discovery API, Leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles, and Open-Meteo for weather. [TBD: if there are only two leads, the room votes one brief out here.]

Clocks start now, and the agent starts first.

Sprint 0, set the target

8 minutes · the agent starts right away

  1. Lead starts the agent on the basic version.
  2. PM fills in For, Problem, and Must do.
  3. Designer sketches the one screen.
  4. QA and Launch pitch in on the person.
  5. Check: Could a stranger say who it’s for?

Press T, then add a line about every 90 seconds. The basic version doesn’t need a problem yet: the camera with tracking drawn on it, a photo in and data out, or events on a map. The agent builds that while the team decides who it’s for, so nobody waits.

Challenges to pose while you walk: “Who exactly? Name a person you know.” “Is that a problem, or did it only happen once?”

Target’s set. Now the real build.

Sprint 1, build the must-do

14 minutes · stay ahead of the agent

  1. PM sends the first real ask.
  2. Designer writes the words on screen.
  3. QA writes three real-input tests.
  4. Launch drafts the pitch.
  5. PM has the next ask ready early.

Press T, then add a line about every three minutes. The rhythm: while the agent builds one thing, the team decides the next. When the agent finishes and there’s no next ask waiting, the team fell behind.

Challenges to pose: “Does this change serve the person on your sheet?” “What did you cut to make room for that?”

Hands off for a few minutes. Let’s look at what we have.

Review, one change each

3 minutes · the agent waits

  1. See the app as it is right now.
  2. Each seat asks for one change.
  3. PM ranks them. The top two go in.
  4. Same seats huddle, 60 seconds.
  5. QA tests another team’s app.

This is a design review the way a company runs one. One change per seat keeps it short. In the huddle, all the PMs meet, all the designers, and so on; steal anything useful. QA brings back what broke on the other team’s app.

Back to work, and this time there’s a deadline.

Sprint 2, polish and freeze

12 minutes · freeze at 3:19

  1. The agent builds the top two changes.
  2. QA runs all three tests for real.
  3. PM cuts whatever won’t finish.
  4. Launch rehearses with the demo path.
  5. Freeze. Nobody touches the keyboard.

Press T, then add a line about every three minutes; the last line goes in at 0:00. Leads: if a change breaks the app in the last three minutes, roll it back. A working smaller app beats a broken bigger one. If an API is down, switch to the saved sample data.

Freeze. Time to launch.

Launch · 2½ minutes each

Show it running.

2:30

Who it’s for, the app running, what’s different for them after. Then one question from the room.

For each team: R resets the clock, T starts it. Switch the projector to that lead’s laptop. One question from the room per team; if nobody asks, ask QA what broke and how they fixed it.

Every team had the same kind of agent. The people decided what got built.
