## In one sentence

Same sentence as last week, but the listener is instructed to be indifferent, distracted, or skeptical, and the skill is holding your ground without getting louder.

## Why this one

The pressure slot changes exactly one variable: the listener. Last week people practised on partners who wanted them to succeed, which is not the room they will actually be in. A recruiter at a table, a professor between classes, and a stranger at a conference all give you a version of indifference, and the reflex responses to it (talking faster, talking louder, apologising, trailing off) all make it worse.

Nobody needs to have been at week 1. Anyone new builds a sentence in the first three minutes of the rep using the same three mechanics.

## Materials

- The three listener roles printed on slips, several sets.
- Timer.
- Last week's three mechanics still on the slide for anyone new.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Dates. October 8 is the send-it session: bring the name and email of one real person you want to reach. Say this twice.
- Anyone new: you need one sentence about what you do. Three mechanics are on the screen and you will build it during the first pass.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **what you would do this weekend if nothing were due.**

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

Explain the three roles. The listener draws one and does not reveal it.

| Role | What the listener does | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **The Indifferent** | Polite, neutral, gives nothing. No nodding, no "mhm", no follow-up question. | Most rooms are this. Silence is not rejection. |
| **The Distracted** | Looks at their phone twice, asks you to repeat one thing, half-listens. | Getting attention back without begging for it. |
| **The Skeptic** | Asks "so what?" once, and "why does that matter?" once. Not rude, just unimpressed. | Having a second and third sentence ready. |

Four rounds, three minutes each, new partner every round, listener redraws every round. Speaker says their sentence and then holds the conversation for ninety seconds against whatever they get.

The one rule for speakers, said out loud before round 1: **when it goes flat, do not go faster and do not go louder. Ask them a question.** That is the entire technique and it takes four rounds to become a reflex.

After round 2, stop for sixty seconds and ask the room to name what they did when it went flat. Most will say "talked more." Then run rounds 3 and 4.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Two volunteers, one at a time, in front of the room. The facilitator plays The Skeptic. Ninety seconds each, no warning about which role is coming.

Room watches for one thing only: **the moment the speaker's pace changed.** Call it out by timestamp. Then two trues and a turn.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Which role was hardest? Almost always The Indifferent, because there is nothing to push against.
2. What did asking a question back actually do? Push to the real answer: it moves the burden of the silence onto them, and it gets you information you can aim at.
3. Where in the next month are you going to meet The Indifferent for real?

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is defending a design in code review, where the reviewer is not hostile, just unconvinced, and the wrong move is exactly the same one: more words, faster.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Before next Thursday, find the name and the email address of one real person outside this club who could move something you want. Bring it to October 8. Do not contact them yet.

Next week is the transfer session: bring the thing you actually want and have been avoiding saying.

## Failure modes

- **Listeners overplay and become rude.** Rude is not the exercise and it teaches nothing. Re-read the role: unimpressed, not hostile. Stop a pair if it turns mean.
- **The room finds it funny for a round and then flat.** That flatness is the content. Name it and keep going.
- **A speaker shuts down.** Swap them to listener for a round. Nobody sits out, and being The Skeptic teaches the same lesson from the other chair.
- **Everyone plays the same role.** Redraw every round and check the slips.
