## In one sentence

Convert a semester of invisible effort into three lines that another person can verify.

## Why this one

Students finish semesters of real work with nothing anyone else can check. The work happened, and then it evaporated, and in April they sit down to write a transfer application or a résumé and have to reconstruct it from memory, badly.

Evidence is the last arc because it needs material, and by mid November there is a semester of it. It is also the arc that feeds Demo Day on December 10, which is the term's public event.

Nothing here is about résumés as documents. It is about the underlying move: making a claim that a stranger can check.

## Materials

- Cards and pens.
- The claim test on a slide.
- Timer.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Results from November 5: how many meetings actually happened. Get the number and say it.
- **No meeting on November 26.** Next after today is November 19, then December 3, then Demo Day on December 10.
- Demo Day: three minutes each, in front of guests. Everyone presents. Say it now so nobody is surprised on December 3.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **something you did this semester that nobody thanked you for.**

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

The claim test, on screen. A line of evidence has three parts, and if any one is missing a stranger cannot use it:

| Part | Question it answers | Failure without it |
|---|---|---|
| **The verb** | What did you actually do? | "Was involved in", "helped with", "participated" |
| **The object** | On what, specifically? | Nobody can picture it |
| **The check** | How would someone verify it? | It is a claim about your character, not your work |

The check is the part everyone omits and the only part that carries weight. A count, a date, a name, a link, a person who would confirm it.

**Minutes 0 to 6, alone.** List ten things you did since August. Not achievements. Things you did. Fixed something, organised something, taught someone, showed up somewhere sixteen times, sat with a friend through a hard week, kept a group project alive.

Ten is deliberately more than people think they have. The last three are usually the good ones.

**Minutes 6 to 14, trios.** Each person reads their list. The other two pick the three that are hardest to see from outside and least likely to be on anyone's radar. Not the impressive ones. The invisible ones.

**Minutes 14 to 18, alone.** Write those three as claims, using all three parts. On the card.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Four volunteers read one claim each.

The room's only job: **say how you would check it.** If nobody can name a check, the claim is not evidence yet and gets rewritten on the spot with the room's help.

Then two trues and a turn.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Which of your ten did you almost not write down? Why?
2. Who had a check available that they never thought of as a check? A group chat, an attendance count, a person who was there.
3. What could you start counting now so that a check exists in January?

Then:

> Nobody is going to come find out what you did. The record is not kept for you anywhere. The people who look like they got lucky in April are the people who wrote things down in November.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is commit messages and a changelog. Same job, same discipline, and most of them say "fixed stuff" and are worth nothing six months later for exactly that reason.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Start a file. Any file, anywhere. Three lines a week, in the claim format. That is the entire habit and it takes ninety seconds.

Next week: telling a story about something that went wrong, in a way that makes people trust you more.

## Failure modes

- **People list achievements and stall at four.** Say it again: not achievements, things you did. Give an example of something small and unimpressive from your own semester.
- **The room deflects with modesty.** Common and worth naming once, plainly: this is a room for accuracy, not for modesty or for boasting, and both of those are ways of avoiding the record.
- **Somebody insists they did nothing this semester.** They enrolled, attended, and survived a term. Start there, with a count, and the rest comes.
- **The three parts turn into a résumé workshop.** It is not one. No formatting, no documents, no bullet points. Claims only.

## Record

Attendance count, how many people wrote three complete claims.
