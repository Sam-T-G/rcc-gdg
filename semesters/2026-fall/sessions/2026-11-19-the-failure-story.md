## In one sentence

Tell something that went wrong so that the listener trusts you more afterwards, not less, and let the room score the trust rather than the polish.

## Why this one

The pressure slot raises the stakes on Evidence by making the evidence unflattering. Every interview, application, and serious conversation asks a version of this question, and most people answer with either a disguised brag or a confession that costs them the room.

The structure that works is teachable in twenty minutes, and once someone has it they stop dreading the question. It is also the most direct preparation for Demo Day, where the honest three minutes always outperform the polished one.

## Materials

- The four beats on a slide.
- Timer.
- Cards.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- **No meeting next week.** No classes November 23 to 29. Next meeting is December 3.
- December 3 is Demo Day prep and the last rehearsal. December 10 is Demo Day: three minutes each, guests in the room, everyone presents.
- Ask now, out loud, who is willing to help invite guests: faculty, the advisor, ACM, other clubs.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **a thing you were completely wrong about.**

Low stakes on purpose. It gets the room used to saying an unflattering true thing in a normal tone of voice.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

The four beats, on screen. In this order, and the order is the whole technique:

1. **What you were trying to do.** Plainly, without defending it. Ten seconds.
2. **What went wrong.** The actual thing, not the circumstances around it. If the sentence contains "but" you are still in beat 1.
3. **What you found out.** The specific thing you did not know before and know now.
4. **What you do differently, with evidence it stuck.** A time you did it differently since. Without beat 4 the whole story is an apology.

The two failure shapes, named out loud so people can hear themselves doing them:

- **The disguised brag.** "My weakness is that I care too much." Nobody has ever believed this. It costs trust rather than building it.
- **The confession.** All of beat 2, none of beats 3 and 4. Makes the listener responsible for your feelings, which is not what they asked for.

**Minutes 0 to 5, on a card, four lines, one per beat.** Something real. Something from this year, ideally this semester.

**Minutes 5 to 18, trios.** Two minutes to tell it, one minute for the other two to respond with one question only:

> Do I trust you more or less than before you told me that, and which beat did it?

Every person tells it twice, to different people, and the second telling is where it improves.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Three volunteers, two minutes each.

The room scores on one axis only: **trust, up or down.** Hands, immediately, before any discussion. Then the room names which beat moved it.

Do not score polish. Say so explicitly before the first volunteer, because the room will drift toward praising delivery and delivery is not what is being trained.

Then two trues and a turn.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Which beat did people skip most? Beat 4, every time. The story feels complete at 3 and it is not.
2. Whose trust went up? What did they do that the others did not?
3. Where does someone ask you this for real in the next six months?

Then:

> The reason this works is that a person who can describe their own mistake precisely is a person who noticed it, which means they will notice the next one. That is the only thing the listener is actually trying to find out.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is a postmortem. Same four beats, written down, blameless by convention, and read by people who were not there.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

- Bring one thing to show on December 10. Anything you did this semester. It does not have to be technical, finished, or impressive.
- Invite one person to Demo Day. A friend, an instructor, someone from another club.
- No meeting November 26. Next is December 3.

## Failure modes

- **Somebody tells something too raw.** Redirect gently to a smaller story before it starts. Ask for something from this semester and academic. The frame is a skill drill, not a disclosure exercise.
- **The room praises delivery.** Cut it and restate the axis. Trust, not polish, every time.
- **Everyone tells the same story about time management.** Ask for one that is specific to them and cost them something. The generic answer is the avoidance.
- **Beat 4 does not exist yet because the failure was recent.** Legitimate. The honest version is "here is what I am doing differently starting now, ask me in January." That builds trust too, and say so.

Attendance count, how many people commit to showing something on December 10, guest invitations promised.
