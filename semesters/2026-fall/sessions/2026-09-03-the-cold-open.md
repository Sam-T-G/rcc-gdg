## In one sentence

Everyone writes down one thing they want this year that they cannot get alone, the cards are shuffled, and each person spends the hour asking the room for something on someone else's behalf.

## Why this one

It is easier to advocate for a stranger than for yourself, and almost nobody notices that about themselves until it happens in a room. This session makes the gap visible in about forty minutes, which is the whole thesis of the semester delivered before anyone has committed to anything. It also works cold: a person who wandered in from the hallway is not behind, because nobody has context.

Running it a week before Club Rush is deliberate. People tell their friends about this one.

## Materials

- Index cards, one per person plus ten spares. Pens.
- A visible timer (phone on the projector is fine).
- A trash bag or a shredder. Cards are destroyed in the room at the end, in front of everyone. Say so before anyone writes.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

Dates only:

- Club Rush is the week of September 8. Confirm the day, time, and table location with Student Activities before this meeting and say it out loud here.
- Next Thursday, same room and time: the session that gives you the line you will say at the Club Rush table.
- Discord QR on the screen for the whole hour.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **a thing you are good at that is not on your transcript.**

Nobody reports back. The point is that every person in the room has used their voice before 2:42.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

Read this aloud, close to word for word.

> On the card in front of you, write one thing you want this year that you cannot get by yourself. Not a goal. A thing another person has to give you. A transfer letter. An internship. Someone to build a project with. A ride to a hackathon. A study group that actually meets. Someone to tell you the truth about your portfolio.
>
> Do not write your name. Nobody will ever know whose card is whose, and every card gets destroyed in this room at the end of the hour.

Ninety seconds to write. Then collect the cards face down, shuffle them thoroughly in front of the room, and deal one back to each person. Anyone who draws their own card swaps it with a neighbour without saying why.

> You are now holding a stranger's want. For the next twelve minutes your job is to find someone in this room who could plausibly move it forward, and ask them. Not explain it. Ask.
>
> You have no idea who wrote this. You cannot be embarrassed on their behalf. Go.

Twelve minutes, everyone on their feet, moving. The facilitator circulates and does two things only: keeps people standing, and when a conversation turns into a discussion about the card, says "make the ask" and walks away.

If the room is smaller than six people, run it in one circle instead: each person reads their card aloud and the room spends two minutes naming who on campus could help.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Ask three people by name, not by volunteer. Each gets sixty seconds to make their card's ask to the whole room, in the first person plural ("someone here needs...").

After each one the room runs **two trues and a turn** (see [meeting-algorithm.md](../../../docs/03-playbooks/meeting-algorithm.md)). The person who made the ask says nothing until all three are given.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

Three questions, in this order. Take two or three answers each, not more.

1. Hands up: who found that easier than expected? Keep them up. Now, who thinks it would have been harder if the card were yours?
2. What exactly was different? Push past "I was less nervous" to the real answer: nothing was at stake for you personally, so you asked plainly. Plainness is the whole skill.
3. Somebody in this room is holding your card. What would you want them to do with it?

Then the reveal, and the pitch for the semester:

> Everything we do this semester is the same move you just made, aimed at your own life instead of a stranger's. Saying what you want without decorating it. Asking someone who can say no. Making sure the work you did is visible to somebody who was not there. None of it is technical, none of it requires a major, and all of it transfers.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version of what you just did is filing an issue on a project you do not own. Telling strangers what is missing, in public, with your name attached. Same nerve, different room.

Destroy the cards now, visibly.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

One action before next Thursday: **say your own want out loud to one person who is not in this room.** Not by text. Out loud.

Then: Discord QR, Bevy chapter QR, and next week's session by name. Tell them next week is the one that arms them for Club Rush and that members are wanted at the table.

## Failure modes

- **Somebody writes a joke card.** Fine, deal it anyway. The person who draws it will improvise and the room will laugh. Do not police the cards.
- **Somebody writes something heavy.** Possible, and the anonymity is what makes it safe. Do not single it out in live fire. Pick the three live-fire cards by asking people who look comfortable.
- **The room sits down and talks.** The rep dies sitting. Keep everyone standing for the full twelve minutes.
- **Cards leave the room.** They do not. Destroy them in front of everyone or the anonymity was a lie.

## Record

In the meeting notes: attendance count, how many people spoke during live fire, Discord joins that day. Counts only, no names.
