What this is

A practice floor for the skills no class assigns.

  • Saywhat you want, without decorating it.
  • Asksomeone who can say no.
  • Showthe work you did to somebody who was not there.

Any major, any level. A first-semester student and someone who has already shipped an app both leave with something.

Discord

Thirty seconds, no more. The room is here to do the thing. The three verbs are the three arcs; do not name the arcs yet.

What this is not

Not a coding club by default.

The weekly meeting is never a coding session. You will never be the least equipped person in this room.

If you code, each session ends by naming the technical version of the skill. Ten seconds. Workshops and hackathons are separate events you opt into.

Thursdays, 2:30 to 3:30 PM. Same room every week.

Discord

A programmer will answer everything technically. Name the rung, then move on. The rung is a door, not a detour. The Modular Impact Sprint and DevFest are the opt-in events; their dates are not confirmed, so do not promise one.

2:42 · The Rep · 18 min

One thing you want this year that you cannot get by yourself.

No name

A transfer letter. An internship. Someone to build a project with. A ride to a hackathon. A study group that actually meets. Someone to tell you the truth about your portfolio.

Not a goal. A thing another person has to give you.

Discord

Read it close to word for word: "On the card in front of you, write one thing you want this year that you cannot get by yourself. Not a goal. A thing another person has to give you. Do not write your name. Nobody will ever know whose card is whose, and every card gets destroyed in this room at the end of the hour."

Ninety seconds. Collect face down, shuffle thoroughly in front of the room, deal one back to each person. Anyone who draws their own card swaps with a neighbour without saying why.

2:45 · The Rep · 12 min · On your feet

You are holding a stranger's want.

Find someone in this room who could plausibly move it forward, and ask them. Not explain it. Ask.

You have no idea who wrote it. You cannot be embarrassed on their behalf. Go.

Discord

Circulate and do two things only: keep people standing, and when a conversation turns into a discussion about the card, say "make the ask" and walk away. The rep dies sitting.

Fewer than six people: one circle instead. Each person reads their card aloud and the room spends two minutes naming who on campus could help.

3:00 · Live fire · 12 min

"Someone here needs..."

Three people. Sixty seconds each. Make the card's ask to the whole room.

Then: two trues and a turn

  • TrueSomething that landed. Specific.
  • TrueA second one.
  • TurnThe one change with the highest payoff.

The speaker says nothing until all three are given.

Discord

Pick three people by name, not by volunteer, and pick people who look comfortable; a heavy card must not be singled out. First person plural: "someone here needs".

Announce the critique by name so the room learns it as a form. "You said the number" beats "that was good". Turns are phrased as turns: "turn the last sentence into a question". No advice unless asked. One voice at a time. Cut anyone who opens with "I would have".

3:12 · Debrief · 10 min

Three questions, in this order

  1. Hands up: who found that easier than expected? Keep them up. Who thinks it would have been harder if the card were yours?
  2. What exactly was different?
  3. Somebody in this room is holding your card. What do you want them to do with it?

Two or three answers each. Not more.

Discord

On question 2, push past "I was less nervous" to the real answer: nothing was at stake for you personally, so you asked plainly. Plainness is the whole skill.
