## In one sentence

Everyone says, to the whole room, the real thing they want and have been avoiding asking for, and the room's only job is to reflect back what it actually heard.

## Why this one

Transfer week points the skill at the student's own life. The first two weeks were a sentence about what you do; this one is a sentence about what you want, which is harder by an order of magnitude and is the actual bottleneck for most students.

The constraint that makes it work is on the listeners, not the speaker: the room reports what it heard and is forbidden from advising. Most people have never had a want reflected back to them without being told what to do about it, and hearing your own ask in someone else's mouth is what exposes the vagueness in it.

## Materials

- Cards and pens.
- Timer.
- The reflect-back frame on a slide: "What I heard you want is `___`. The person who can give it to you is `___`."

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- **October 8 is next week and it is the send session.** You will write and send one real message from this room. Bring a name and an email address. Anyone without one gets fifteen minutes at the start of next week to find one, but come with it if you can.
- Demo Day is December 10. Say it every week from here.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **the best thing that has happened to you since Thursday.**

Keep this one light. The rep that follows is heavy and the warm rep should not foreshadow it.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

Frame it plainly, without ceremony:

> Most of you are carrying something you want that you have not asked anyone for. Usually because asking would make it real, or because you think you have not earned the right yet, or because you do not know who to ask. Today you say it out loud in a room of people who cannot grade you.

Five minutes, silent, on a card:

1. The thing I want.
2. Who could plausibly give it to me. A role is fine if you do not have a name.
3. What I have been telling myself about why I have not asked.

Line 3 stays private. It is on the card so the person is honest with themselves, and it never gets read aloud. Say that before they write.

Then trios, twelve minutes. Each person reads lines 1 and 2 only. The other two respond with the frame on the slide and nothing else:

> What I heard you want is `___`. The person who can give it to you is `___`.

No advice. No "have you tried." No encouragement. If a listener starts advising, the speaker says "reflect it back" and they start over. Practise that phrase once as a room before starting, so it is available to everyone.

Each person gets four minutes. Speaker goes last in reacting, and their only job is to say whether the reflection matched.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Three volunteers, in front of the room. Each reads lines 1 and 2.

The room reflects back, one voice at a time, three reflections per person. Then the speaker says one sentence: what was different between what they said and what came back.

That gap is the whole session. Name it every time.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Whose want came back smaller than they said it? Whose came back bigger?
2. Who realised mid-sentence that they were asking the wrong person?
3. Line 3 on your card, the reason you have not asked: is it still true after saying the thing out loud?

Then:

> A want that another person cannot repeat back is not an ask yet. It is a feeling. Next week you turn one of these into a message and send it.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is writing an issue somebody else can act on. If the reader cannot restate what you need, the issue sits open for a year regardless of how real the problem is.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Bring to next week: the name, the email address, and the one thing you are asking for. Written down. Next week is writing and sending, not deciding.

## Failure modes

- **The room advises.** It will, constantly, and it is meant kindly. Cut it every time. The prohibition is the entire mechanism.
- **Someone shares something genuinely heavy.** Reflect it back exactly as the frame says and move on at the same pace as everyone else. Do not make it a moment. The frame protects them; special treatment does not. If someone needs more than the room can give, the advisor and RCC student services exist and an organizer can point to them afterward, privately.
- **Somebody has nothing they want.** They have something they are avoiding. Ask what they would ask for if a yes were guaranteed, then remove the guarantee.
- **A person will not read theirs aloud.** They read it in the trio only. That is enough to count and nobody is drafted for live fire.

## Record

Attendance count, how many people leave with a name and an email for next week.
