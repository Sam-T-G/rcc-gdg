## In one sentence

Every person answers "so what do you do?" three times, with five fewer seconds each round, until only the load-bearing words are left.

## Why this one

Every student gets asked this question by a stranger who can help them, and most answers are a list of courses. Compression is the fastest teacher there is: you cannot pad an eight-second answer, so the round strips out everything that was there for the speaker's comfort rather than the listener's use.

It runs during Club Rush week on purpose. People walk out of this session with a sentence they will use at the table two days later, which is also how the club fills its own tabling shifts.

## Materials

- Index cards and pens, one per person.
- A visible countdown timer, large enough to read from the back.
- The three rules written on the board or a single slide. That is the whole deck.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Club Rush: day, time, table location. Ask for tabling shifts by name, two people per shift, sign-ups on the board during the break at 3:27.
- Next Thursday the real programming starts and the first arc runs four weeks.
- Discord QR up for the hour.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **the last thing you looked up.**

No report-back.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

Put the three rules on the screen and leave them there:

1. **Name the person you help, not the field you are in.** "I help people who cannot read their own medical bills" beats "I am pre-nursing."
2. **One concrete noun or number.** Not "a lot of experience." A thing that exists or a count.
3. **End on something they can grab.** The last five words should make a follow-up question obvious.

Then three rounds. New partner each round. The listener asks "so what do you do?" and says nothing else until the timer ends.

| Round | Each way | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 seconds | Most people fill it and say nothing. That is the baseline. |
| 2 | 12 seconds | The course list goes first. Good. |
| 3 | 8 seconds | Only the load-bearing words survive. |

Between rounds, thirty seconds of silence to cut, not to rewrite. Say it out loud: **you are deleting, not editing.**

After round 3, ninety seconds to write the eight-second version on the card. That card goes in a pocket, not a phone. It is the receipt.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Four volunteers. Each says their eight-second version to the whole room, twice in a row.

The room does not run two trues and a turn here. Instead, after all four, one question: **which of those made you want to ask a follow-up question, and what would you have asked?**

That is the only measure that matters, and it is the listener's call, not the speaker's.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. What did you cut in round 2 that you thought was essential in round 1?
2. Who ended up describing a person rather than a subject? How did it feel to say?
3. Whose answer would work on someone who has never heard of your major?

Then:

> Nobody in this room is going to be judged on how much they know by a stranger who has twenty seconds. They are going to be judged on whether the twenty seconds made them curious. That is a skill, it is learnable, and you just learned it in forty minutes.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version of this is the first paragraph of a README. The same job, the same compression, and most of them are bad for exactly the same reason.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Two things before next Thursday:

1. Use the card on one stranger. A professor, a person at the Club Rush table next to yours, anybody.
2. Tabling shifts. Names on the board now.

Say next week's session by name and that the four-week arc starts then.

## Failure modes

- **Round 1 runs long because people are enjoying it.** Cut it exactly on time. The compression only works if the clock is real.
- **Someone refuses to cut anything.** Give them a fourth round at five seconds. It resolves itself.
- **The room treats it as an elevator pitch exercise.** It is not a pitch. Say so: a pitch sells, this makes a stranger curious. Different jobs.
- **Nobody signs up to table.** Ask three specific people by name before the room disperses. Open volunteering fails; direct asks do not.

## Record

Attendance count, tabling shifts filled, how many people wrote a card. Counts only.
