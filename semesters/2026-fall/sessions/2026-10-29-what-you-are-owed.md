## In one sentence

Bring the real negotiation you are avoiding, a grade, a schedule, a role, a workload, an offer, and rehearse it against someone playing the other side.

## Why this one

Transfer week points the Ask arc at the student's own situation, and this is the one where the stakes are already real. Community college students carry an unusual number of these: a units cap, a prerequisite waiver, a financial aid appeal, a work schedule that collides with a required lab, a group project where they are doing four people's work.

Most go unasked because students believe the terms are fixed. Many of them are not, and the ones that are fixed become obvious quickly once you ask. Rehearsing against a person who pushes back is what converts knowing that into doing it.

## Materials

- Cards and pens.
- The four properties from week 5 still on the slide.
- Timer.
- Printed list of who at RCC actually decides what (below). Verify it before the meeting.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- **November 5 is next week and it is the booking session.** Bring the name of a person with power over something you want, and their email or office hours. You will send the invite from the room.
- Demo Day December 10. The Evidence arc starts November 12.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **something you are currently doing more of than your share of.**

Half the room will surface their negotiation while answering this.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

**Minutes 0 to 5, on a card, silently:**

1. The situation. One sentence.
2. What I actually want out of it. One sentence, and a number if there is one.
3. Who decides. A role is acceptable if you do not have a name.
4. What I would accept instead. This line is the difference between a request and an ultimatum.

Line 4 is the one people skip. Say out loud that a negotiation without a fallback is not a negotiation, it is a demand, and demands from students to institutions lose.

**Minutes 5 to 18, pairs, two rounds of six minutes.** Speaker describes the situation in thirty seconds, then makes the ask. Partner plays the other side with three instructions:

- Do not be a villain. Be a busy person with rules and a queue.
- Push back at least twice: once on whether it is possible, once on why this person specifically.
- Give ground only if the ask is small, specific, dated, and exitable.

Swap.

**Who decides what at RCC.** Print and hand out, and confirm each line before the meeting rather than trusting this file:

| Want | Who to actually ask |
|---|---|
| Prerequisite waiver, course substitution | Department chair or the discipline faculty, not the front counter |
| Late add, unit overload, schedule exception | Counselor first, then Admissions and Records |
| Financial aid appeal | Financial Aid office, in writing, with the appeal form |
| Grade dispute | The instructor first, always, and in writing before anything escalates |
| Recommendation letter | The instructor, four weeks out, with your materials attached |
| Accommodations | DSPS (Disability Support Programs and Services) |
| Club room, funding, event approval | Student Activities and the ICC representative |

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Two volunteers, in front of the room. The facilitator plays the other side and plays it well: busy, not hostile, bound by a rule they did not write.

Three minutes each, then two trues and a turn. The room watches for the moment the speaker either names their fallback or fails to.

If neither volunteer has a fallback, do a third round with the facilitator asking directly for one. Watching someone construct a fallback live is the most useful thing that can happen in this session.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Who found out mid-rehearsal that they were about to ask the wrong person?
2. Who has a fallback they would actually be happy with? That is often the real ask, and it was available the whole time.
3. What is the worst realistic outcome of asking? Not the worst imaginable. The worst realistic one.
4. What does it cost to ask in writing rather than in person, and which does your situation want?

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is scoping a ticket down instead of accepting the whole thing. The move is the same one: not "no", not "yes to all of it", but "here is the version of this that works."

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Next week you book the real appointment. Bring a name, an email or office hours, and one sentence about what the meeting is for.

## Failure modes

- **A situation is genuinely serious** (harassment, discrimination, a Title IX matter, financial crisis). This is a club meeting and not the venue. Take it out of the room, privately, and point to the advisor and to RCC student services. Do not rehearse it as an exercise.
- **Partners play villains.** Kills the rep. Re-read the three instructions: busy person with rules, not an antagonist.
- **Somebody has no negotiation.** Give them a real one from campus life: office hours that collide with a class, a group project split, a waitlist. Or make them the partner for both rounds.
- **The room decides the institution is immovable.** Some of it is. Point at the table: those are the doors that are open, and most students in the room have never walked through any of them.
