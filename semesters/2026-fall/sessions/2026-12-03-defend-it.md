## In one sentence

Bring one decision you made this semester, let the room attack it, and either hold it with reasons or update it out loud, because refusing to do either is the only losing move.

## Why this one

Transfer week aims Evidence at the student's own judgment. A person who can only list what they did is describing labour. A person who can say why they chose it, and what they gave up to choose it, is describing judgment, and judgment is what gets someone into a program, a role, or a room.

This is also the last working session before Demo Day. The defence people practise here is what carries a three-minute presentation when a guest asks a question at the end.

## Materials

- The decision card format on a slide.
- Timer.
- The Demo Day slot list, printed, to be filled in during The Ask.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- **Demo Day is next Thursday, December 10, this room, this time.** Guests confirmed: [TBD]. Everyone presents for three minutes.
- Slots get assigned today before anyone leaves.
- Retrospective and handoff PR opens December 4, per the semester calendar.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **a decision you made this semester that you are still not sure about.**

Most people will use their warm rep answer as their card. That is fine and saves five minutes.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

The decision card, four lines:

1. **The decision.** What you chose. One sentence.
2. **The alternative you rejected.** The real one, the one that was actually tempting. If you cannot name a genuine alternative, this was not a decision and you need a different card.
3. **Why, at the time.** What you knew then. Not what you know now.
4. **What would have changed your mind.** The specific piece of information that would have flipped it.

Line 4 is the one that separates this from a justification. It is also the hardest and worth saying so.

**Minutes 0 to 5, write the card.** Anything: a major, a class you dropped, a job you took, a project you abandoned, a group you left, a transfer target, how you spent your summer.

**Minutes 5 to 18, trios, four minutes each.** Speaker reads lines 1 to 3. The other two attack, and they have three angles, one each and then whichever fits:

- **The cost:** what did this cost you that you have not admitted?
- **The alternative:** why was the other one worse? Make them say it.
- **The hindsight:** if you were choosing again today, same choice?

Speaker's job, and the only two acceptable outcomes: **hold it with a reason, or update it out loud.** "I still think it was right and here is why" is a win. "I would choose differently now and here is what changed" is a win. Restating the decision more firmly without a reason is the loss, and the attackers call it when they see it.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Two volunteers. The room attacks, one voice at a time, three minutes each.

The facilitator asks the final question both times: **what would have changed your mind?**

The room scores one thing: did they hold with a reason, update, or neither? Then two trues and a turn.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Who updated? What did that cost? Usually nothing, which is the point.
2. Who found their alternative was not real, that they had already decided and were describing it afterwards as a choice?
3. What is the difference between defending a decision and defending yourself? The room can usually name this once they have felt it.

Then:

> Nobody at Demo Day will ask whether you worked hard. They will ask why you did it that way. The answer "it seemed right" is the same as no answer. "Here is what I knew and here is what would have changed my mind" is the answer that makes people take you seriously, and it is available to everybody in this room right now.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is an architecture decision record. Context, the options, the choice, the consequences, written down at the time. Teams that keep them stop re-arguing the same decision every six months.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

**Demo Day slots.** Fill the printed list now, in the room, name against slot. Everyone gets three minutes.

The format for next week, said out loud so nobody over-prepares:

- One minute: what you did. Use the claim format from November 12.
- One minute: one decision inside it and why. Use today's card.
- One minute: what you would do next, or what you would do differently.

No slides required. Bring the thing if it exists. If it does not exist as an object, say it anyway.

Invite the person you promised to invite.

## Failure modes

- **Attacks turn personal.** Cut immediately. The target is the decision. Restate the three angles.
- **A speaker will not update on anything.** Name it plainly: "you have restated it twice without a reason." That is the coaching, and it is why the room is there.
- **Somebody has no real decision.** Everyone chose their schedule. Start there.
- **Slots do not get filled.** Do not let the room disperse. Go person by person out loud. Anyone genuinely unwilling can co-present with someone, but nobody opts out silently.
