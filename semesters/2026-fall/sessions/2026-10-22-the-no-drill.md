## In one sentence

You make an ask, you get refused, and the skill being drilled is the sentence that comes after the no.

## Why this one

Every student knows what to do with a yes. Almost nobody has a second sentence ready for a no, so the conversation ends there and the want is abandoned. In practice most first answers are a soft no that is really "not like that" or "not me", and the person who has one more sentence gets a redirect, a name, or a smaller yes.

Practising it against people instructed to refuse removes the sting from the real one. That is the whole design: make the no boring.

## Materials

- Refusal slips, three kinds, several sets (below).
- The three recoveries on a slide.
- Timer. This session is entirely timed rounds.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Two weeks to November 5, when everyone books a real appointment. Start thinking about who.
- Demo Day December 10, and the Evidence arc that leads to it starts November 12.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **a time you were told no and it turned out fine.**

This primes the session honestly and takes ninety seconds of weight off the room.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

The three refusals. The refuser draws one and plays it straight:

| Refusal | The line | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| **The soft no** | "I don't think I have time for that right now." | Usually "not that much time." Try smaller. |
| **The wrong door** | "That's not really my area." | Often true. They know who it is. |
| **The flat no** | "No." | Sometimes final. Ask what would change it, then leave well. |

The three recoveries, on screen. One per refusal, and getting the match right is the skill:

1. **Shrink it.** "Would fifteen minutes on just the one question work better?"
2. **Ask for the name.** "Who would you point me to instead?"
3. **Ask what would change it, then close clean.** "Is there a version of this you would say yes to? If not, no problem, thank you for the straight answer."

Six rounds, ninety seconds each, new partner every two rounds, refusers redraw every round. Ask, get refused, recover. Then swap chairs.

Stop after round 3 for sixty seconds. One question to the room: **how many of you apologised?** Most hands go up. Name it: apologising after a no converts a normal negotiation into an imposition and teaches the other person they did something to you. Cut it. Then run rounds 4 to 6.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Three volunteers, one at a time. The facilitator refuses, drawing without telling them which.

Two passes each. Ninety seconds, and the volunteer must get to a second and a third sentence. The facilitator escalates: refuse the recovery too, at least once.

The room watches for one thing: **did the recovery match the refusal?** Shrinking an ask against a wrong-door refusal wastes the exchange. Then two trues and a turn.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Which refusal was hardest to stay in the room for?
2. Who got a redirect out of a no? That is the most common real outcome and almost nobody expects it.
3. What is the actual cost of the third sentence? Push until the room says it: nothing. There is no cost. The fear is of a feeling, not a consequence.
4. Where is a real no you took as final that was probably a soft no?

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is a rejected pull request you do not abandon. The maintainer said no to that version, which is different from no to you, and the people who ship are the ones who send the second version.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Go back to one no you have already received this year and send the second sentence. Report it in Discord either way.

Next week: the transfer session, where the ask is about something you are actually owed. Bring the real situation.

## Failure modes

- **Refusers soften and say yes.** The most common failure. Say up front, twice: your job is to refuse, every time, no exceptions. A refuser who caves has wasted their partner's round.
- **The room gets glum.** Six rounds of refusal is heavy by round 4. Keep pace fast, keep it loud, and land the round-3 apology moment for the laugh it will get.
- **Somebody takes it personally.** Swap them to refuser for two rounds.
- **Recoveries become formulaic.** Fine at first. It is a drill, and fluency comes before nuance.

## Record

Attendance count, and how many people report sending a second sentence in the following week.
