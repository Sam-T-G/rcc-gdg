## In one sentence

Everyone writes and sends one real message to one real person outside the club, in the room, before they leave.

## Why this one

This is the receipt that makes the Voice arc real. Three weeks of practice with no send is three weeks of theatre. The send happens in the room because the gap between a drafted message and a sent one is where almost everyone stalls, and that gap closes only with a timer and witnesses.

Phones and laptops are allowed today, for this only. It is the one session where a device is the point.

## Materials

- Wi-fi confirmed working in A-210 before the meeting. Check it the day before.
- The four-line template on a slide, up the whole hour.
- A wall or whiteboard for the sent tally.
- `FLOAT-1` and `FLOAT-2` free during the rep to read drafts on request. They are not running the room, so they can sit with one person.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

Short and direct:

- Everyone sends today. If you do not have a person, the first fifteen minutes are for finding one and it will work.
- Next week starts the Ask arc: how to make a request that is easy to say yes to.
- Demo Day is December 10.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **who you are writing to and what you want from them.**

This one is not a throwaway. Saying the target out loud commits people before they open a laptop.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

**Minutes 0 to 4, for anyone without a target.** Find one now. Options that always work: a professor whose class you took, an RCC alum on LinkedIn who transferred where you want to go, the author of something you read, a person at a company doing the thing you described three weeks ago, a campus office you have never contacted. Neighbours help. Nobody is allowed to still be searching at minute 4.

**Minutes 4 to 14, write it.** The template, and no message goes over five sentences:

1. **Who you are, in one line.** The sentence from week 1. This is what it was for.
2. **The specific thing you noticed about them.** Not flattery. Evidence you actually looked. One clause.
3. **The ask, small and dated.** Fifteen minutes, one question answered, one document read. Something they can do in a sitting.
4. **The easy exit.** "If you are not the right person, I would take a name instead." This is what makes a cold message answerable, and it is the line everyone wants to cut.

Rules on the slide: five sentences maximum. No apologising for their time. No "I know you're busy." Do not attach anything.

**Minutes 14 to 18, swap.** Read a neighbour's draft against four checks and nothing else: is the ask small, is it dated, is there an exit, is it under five sentences.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Not volunteers this week. Everybody.

Ten minutes on the clock, visible, counting down. The room writes and sends. The facilitator says almost nothing. `FLOAT-1` and `FLOAT-2` circulate for anyone who wants a last read.

As each person sends, they put a mark on the wall. No names on the wall, just marks. The tally builds in front of everyone and it is the most effective pressure in the session.

At two minutes remaining, say it once: "Two minutes. Nothing about this message gets better in the next two minutes."

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Hands up: who sent? (Count it. This is the semester's cleanest metric.)
2. Who nearly cut the exit line? Why? The exit is what makes a cold ask survivable for both people.
3. What did you almost apologise for, and did you cut it?
4. What happens if they do not reply?

Answer that last one directly, because it is the fear under the whole arc:

> Most will not reply. That is the normal outcome and it costs you nothing. The ones who do reply change something. This is a numbers game that nobody tells students is a numbers game, and the only way to lose it is to send zero.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is opening a pull request on a repository you do not own. Same nerve, same odds, same correct response to silence.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

- Report replies in Discord, including the ones that are a no. Especially those.
- If you get no reply in ten days, send one follow-up. One. We will write it in the Ask arc.
- Next week: Anatomy of an Ask.

## Failure modes

- **Wi-fi is down.** Session dies. Check the day before and have a phone hotspot ready.
- **Someone drafts and does not send.** This is the failure mode and it will happen. Sit with them for the last two minutes. Do not shame anyone; the wall tally does enough work on its own.
- **A message is a bad idea** (angry, aimed at someone inappropriate, asking for far too much). This is why `FLOAT-1` and `FLOAT-2` circulate. Redirect quietly to a smaller ask.
- **Someone wants to send to a person they already know well.** Allow it, but push for a stranger. The arc is about someone who has no reason to listen.
- **The room is quiet and it feels like it is not working.** Ten minutes of a room silently sending cold messages is what success looks like here.

## Record

**Attendance count and sent count.** These two numbers are the Voice arc's result. Put both in the meeting notes and in `retrospective.md` at the end of the term.
