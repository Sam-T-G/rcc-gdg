## In one sentence

Everyone books a real appointment with a real person in a position of power, in the room, before they leave.

## Why this one

The Ask arc's receipt. The October 8 send proved people can contact a stranger; this one proves they can get onto the calendar of someone who decides things, which is a different and larger step.

Office hours are the most underused resource at any college. They are staffed, scheduled, empty, and free, and the students who use them get outcomes that look like luck from the outside. Booking one is a five-minute action that most students never take in two years.

## Materials

- Wi-fi confirmed the day before.
- The invite template on a slide.
- Wall or whiteboard for the booked tally.
- The who-decides-what handout from week 7.
- `FLOAT-1` and `FLOAT-2` free to read drafts.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Everyone books today.
- November 12 starts the Evidence arc, four weeks, ending at Demo Day on December 10.
- **There is no meeting on November 26.** No classes November 23 to 29. Say it now and again on November 19.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **who you are booking and what the meeting is for.**

Anyone who cannot finish this sentence gets the first four minutes of the rep to fix it.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

**Minutes 0 to 4, targets.** Anyone without one picks now. Every student in this room has all of these available and has probably used none of them:

- Any instructor whose class you are in. They hold posted hours and are usually alone during them.
- A counselor, for anything about transfer, units, or a plan.
- The department chair of the thing you want to do.
- Whoever runs the campus program you keep meaning to look into.
- The person who replied to your October 8 message.
- Your GDG advisor.

**Minutes 4 to 12, write the request.** Four lines, and no more:

1. Who you are and which of their classes or offices you came through.
2. What the meeting is for, in one sentence. Specific enough that they can prepare.
3. Two concrete times you can do, taken from their posted hours.
4. The exit: "If office hours are easier, I will come to those instead."

Line 3 does the work. "Are you free sometime?" makes them do the scheduling; two times makes it a yes or a no.

**Minutes 12 to 18, swap and check.** Read a neighbour's draft against four checks only: is the purpose specific, are there two real times, is it under five sentences, is there an exit.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Everybody, again. Ten minutes on a visible countdown. The room sends. Marks go on the wall as invites go out.

A booking counts if the request was sent. A confirmed time is better and will mostly arrive later in the week.

`FLOAT-1` and `FLOAT-2` circulate. At two minutes: "Two minutes. Send the one you have."

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Count the wall. Say the number out loud.
2. Who is booking with someone they have been avoiding for more than a month? How long exactly?
3. What did you assume about how busy they are, and where did that assumption come from?

Then land the arc:

> Four weeks ago the exercise was saying what you want out loud in a room that could not do anything about it. Today you asked someone who can. Nothing about you changed in four weeks. The only thing that changed is that you have done it before.

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is asking a maintainer a question in public, on their issue tracker, under your own name, where everyone can see you not know something.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

- Go to the meeting. Report in Discord what came of it, including the ones that fell through.
- No meeting November 26.
- November 12 starts Evidence: making the work you have done visible to people who were not there. Bring nothing; it starts from scratch.

## Failure modes

- **Wi-fi down.** Session dies. Check the day before, have a hotspot.
- **Nobody wants to book with an instructor.** Common. Start the room on the advisor or a counselor, which is lower stakes, and the instructors follow.
- **A student books something they will not attend.** Ask in Discord the following week and count the attends, not the sends. Names stay out of the notes.
- **Somebody says they have nothing to meet about.** "I am trying to work out whether to transfer to X or Y and I would like ten minutes of your read" is a complete and excellent reason, and it is true for most of the room.

## Record

**Attendance count and booked count**, and the following week, how many meetings actually happened. Those three numbers are the Ask arc's result and belong in `retrospective.md`.
