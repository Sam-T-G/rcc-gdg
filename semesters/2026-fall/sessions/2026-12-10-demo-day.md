## In one sentence

Every member gets three minutes in front of a room containing outsiders to show one thing they did this semester, why they did it that way, and what comes next.

## Why this one

The Evidence arc's receipt and the semester's outward-facing rep. It is deliberately not a project showcase: a student who organised something, taught someone, or held a group together presents on the same terms as a student who built an app. The subject is the evidence and the judgment, not the artifact.

This is the last meeting of the term. It also satisfies the [demo-day playbook](../../../docs/03-playbooks/demo-day.md), one of the four Bevy events in goal 2, and produces the attendance number that goes in `retrospective.md`.

Note the difference from Spring 2026's Demo Day, which showed team projects from a build cycle. This one shows individual evidence from a soft-skills semester. Say that to guests when you open, or they will arrive expecting demos.

## The day

This session replaces the standard block. It is the one week the algorithm bends, and the reason is that the whole hour is live fire.

| Clock | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 2:25 | Room set: chairs facing front, projector up, running order visible, check-in live | `LEAD`, `NOTE` |
| 2:30 | Open. Ninety seconds: what this club did this semester and what the next three minutes will look like. Name the arcs. Tell guests it is not a project showcase. | `LEAD` |
| 2:32 | Presentations begin. Three minutes each, hard timer, visible to the presenter. Thirty seconds of questions between. | `TIME` on the clock, `LEAD` asks the first question |
| 3:18 | Close. The numbers out loud: messages sent October 8, appointments booked November 5, meetings held, people who presented today. | `LEAD` |
| 3:24 | Spring: dates, elections, what is coming. One ask: come back in February. | `LEAD` |
| 3:27 | Check-in sweep, photos with consent, room reset | `NOTE` |

Fifteen presenters at three and a half minutes each fills the hour exactly. If more than fifteen sign up, cut questions and run three minutes flat. If the room is small, extend questions to a minute rather than padding the presentations.

**The format, on the screen the whole time:**

1. One minute: what you did. The claim format from November 12: verb, object, check.
2. One minute: one decision inside it, and why. The card from December 3.
3. One minute: what is next, or what you would do differently.

**The rules, said once at the start:**

- The timer is real and it is visible to the presenter. It cuts you off. That is the exercise.
- No slides required and none expected. Bring the thing if it exists.
- Nothing here has to be technical, finished, or impressive.
- Applause after every single person, including the ones who ran short.

**Questions.** `LEAD` asks the first one every time, so the room never sits in silence in front of a presenter. `TIME` does nothing but run the clock; splitting those two jobs is why Demo Day needs a separate timekeeper seat. Use one of:

- What would have changed your mind?
- What did that cost you?
- What is the check on that?

Those are the three questions the semester trained. Guests will pick them up by the fourth presenter.

## The close

Read the numbers out loud. They are the semester's argument and most of the room has never heard them assembled:

- Meetings held out of the meeting days available.
- Cold messages sent from the room on October 8, and replies.
- Appointments booked on November 5, and how many happened.
- People who presented today.
- Guests in the room.

Then, briefly, and without inflating it:

> None of this was technical. Every person here practised saying what they wanted, asking someone who could refuse them, and putting their own work on the record. That transfers into every major in this room, and for the people who want the next rung, we named it every single week.

Then spring: the first meeting date, elections, and the Solution Challenge kickoff.

## Failure modes

- **Presentations run long.** The single most likely failure. The timer is visible and it is honoured from presenter one, or it is honoured from nobody. Cut the first over-runner politely and the rest self-correct.
- **Guests arrive expecting demos.** Handled in the open. Say it in the first ninety seconds.
- **Somebody freezes.** Facilitator asks the first question early and turns it into a conversation. Everybody claps. Move on at the same pace.
- **Fewer presenters show than signed up.** Fill with the questions. Never fill with an unplanned organizer talk.
- **The room is smaller than hoped.** Run it identically. The presenters are the point, not the audience size, and the numbers still go in the retrospective.

## Record

Attendance, guest count, presenter count, Bevy registered against checked-in. These are the semester's closing numbers.
