## In one sentence

Learn the four properties that make a request easy to say yes to, by rewriting five genuinely terrible asks into good ones.

## Why this one

The Voice arc got people saying what they want. This arc is about the person on the other end. A request fails far more often because it was expensive to grant than because the answer was no, and students almost never get taught the difference.

Rewriting other people's bad asks is the fastest way in. It is diagnostic rather than personal, so nobody defends their own wording, and the properties become obvious in about ten minutes.

## Materials

- The five bad asks, printed, one set per trio (below).
- The four properties on a slide.
- Timer.

## 2:30 Open (5 min)

- Replies from October 8: ask for a count of sends that got a response, and for one person to read a reply aloud if they are willing. A no read aloud is worth more here than a yes.
- Arc 2 is four weeks and ends November 5 with everybody booking a real appointment with someone who has power over something they want.
- Demo Day December 10.

## 2:35 Warm rep (7 min)

Pairs, sixty seconds each way: **the last time someone asked you for something and you said yes immediately. Why was it easy?**

Every answer will contain at least one of the four properties. Do not tell them that yet.

## 2:42 The Rep (18 min)

The four properties, on screen:

| Property | Test | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Small** | Can they do it in one sitting? | Effort is what people decline, not you |
| **Specific** | Do they know what "done" looks like? | Vague asks require them to design the work |
| **Dated** | Is there a by-when? | An undated ask is an open-ended obligation |
| **Exitable** | Is there a graceful no? | An ask with no exit is pressure, and pressure gets silence |

Then trios with the printed set. Twelve minutes, roughly two per ask. For each: name which properties are missing, then rewrite it so all four are present.

**The five bad asks:**

1. "Hi Professor, I was wondering if you could help me with my career goals. Let me know if you have time to chat sometime!"
2. "Hey, I saw you work at a company I'm interested in. Would love to pick your brain about the industry and get advice on breaking in."
3. "Can someone in this Discord look over my portfolio and tell me what they think?"
4. "I would really appreciate it if you could write me a letter of recommendation."
5. "Would you be willing to mentor me?"

Ask 5 is the interesting one. It usually cannot be rewritten, only replaced: nobody agrees to an open-ended relationship with a stranger, but many people will answer one question. Let a trio discover that rather than saying it.

Last three minutes: each trio picks its best rewrite of ask 4, the recommendation letter, since that is the one every person in the room will actually need.

## 3:00 Live fire (12 min)

Three trios read their original and their rewrite. The room votes with hands on which rewrite they would grant if they were the recipient, and one person says why.

Then two trues and a turn on the rewrite.

Close live fire on the recommendation letter specifically, and land the point directly: a letter request that includes the deadline, the program, a reminder of what you did in their class, and an easy no is granted far more often than one that does not, and the difference is about six sentences of work by the asker instead of an hour of guessing by the professor.

## 3:12 Debrief and rung (10 min)

1. Which property was missing most often? Usually dated, and it is the cheapest one to add.
2. Which felt rude to include? Almost always the exit. Push on this: an exit is not an invitation to say no, it is what makes the yes voluntary, and a voluntary yes is the only kind that gets honoured.
3. Look at the message you sent last week. Which property was missing?

**The rung, ten seconds:** the technical version is a bug report that gets fixed. Small, specific, reproducible, and it does not make the maintainer guess what you want. The bad ones fail for the same four reasons.

## 3:22 The Ask (5 min)

Rewrite the October 8 message as if you were sending it today, and bring it. Next week you make asks out loud and get refused on purpose.

## Failure modes

- **The room competes on cleverness.** The rewrites should be plain and short. Cut anything performing.
- **Somebody argues the exit is weak.** Take the argument seriously and answer it with the yes-is-voluntary point. Do not wave it away; half the room quietly agrees with them.
- **Trios finish in six minutes.** They skipped the diagnosis. Send them back to name the missing properties before rewriting.
- **No one got a reply to their October 8 message.** Say so plainly and keep going. Ten days is early, and the arc has an answer for silence next week.

## Record

Attendance count, replies reported from the October 8 sends.
